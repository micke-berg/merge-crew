# Merge Crew — character and art brief

## What the game is

Merge Crew is a free browser game that teaches git. The player is the lead developer of a small crew of robot coding agents. They all work in the same codebase, each on its own branch, and they make the mistakes real AI coding agents make: overwriting other people's work, drifting far away from everyone else, never saving their work. The player fixes those mistakes, and later sets rules that prevent them.

The tone is funny, warm and a bit chaotic, never mean. The robots are lovable screw-ups, not villains. The players are developers and people learning to code, from teens to adults.

## How the game looks

The git history is drawn like a metro map: coloured lines (branches) with stops (commits) on a calm background. It is flat 2D with soft shadows. The robots travel along these lines and hop onto each new stop they create. They talk in speech bubbles. A command box sits at the bottom of the screen.

The map is calm and clean. The characters are the most expressive and colourful thing on the screen.

## The crew

These are personalities, not design orders.

- **Tidy:** calm, organised, helpful. The teacher who gives hints.
- **Blaze:** fast, overconfident, impatient. Rewrites history and pushes over other robots' work.
- **Drift:** dreamy and distracted, wanders off. Never keeps in sync with the others.
- **Hoarder:** anxious, keeps everything, can't let go. Never saves its work properly, hides things away and forgets them.

Each one should be recognisable from its shape and pose alone, not only from its colour.

## Creative freedom

You choose the style, shapes, proportions, materials and how "robot" is expressed. Surprise us. As reference points for charm and range of poses (not for copying): toy-like flat characters with many poses, such as Kenney's Toon Characters, and small expressive pixel-art robots.

## What the game needs

1. **Readable at small and large sizes.** About 64 px tall on the map, and still good at about 300 px in speech-bubble portraits.
2. **Side view, facing right.** We mirror for left. Same scale and ground line for all four.
3. **Poses or animations for every robot:**
   - idle
   - move (walk, roll or hover, your choice)
   - hop
   - talk
   - happy or celebrate
   - scared or panic
   - guilty ("oops")
   - thinking

   Each robot also gets one signature action that fits its personality, in your interpretation.
4. **Transparent background**, delivered in one of two forms. Tell us which you chose.
   - PNG sprite sheets: equal frame size, frames in one row, frame count and size listed per animation.
   - Layered SVG parts that we can animate in code.
5. **One crew, one world.** All four clearly belong together.
6. **Rights.** We must be allowed to publish the images in a public open-source repository and in a free game. Tell us which tool made them and its usage terms.

## Process

1. **First, before making full sets,** show three clearly different style directions. Each direction is two images:
   - all four robots standing together
   - a mock game screen: metro-style history lines, the robots on them, one speech bubble, the command box at the bottom

   Then wait for a choice.
2. **Then** make the full set in the chosen direction.

## Please avoid

Copying existing characters or brands, text inside the art, and anything scary or aggressive.
